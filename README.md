## 🧮 Mathematical Modeling: Laplace Pressure Equilibrium

## 🧮 Mathematical Modeling: Laplace Pressure Equilibrium

To verify that a monolayer graphene dome can sustain an internal atmosphere against the hard vacuum of space ($P_{\text{vacuum}} = 0$), we implement the fundamental Laplace thin-walled shell equation:

$$F_{\text{pressure}} = \Delta P \cdot \pi R^2$$
$$F_{\text{resistance}} = \sigma \cdot 2\pi R \cdot t$$

Equating forces for a dome of radius $R = 1\text{ m}$ at an internal metabolic pressure of $\Delta P = 30,397\text{ Pa}$ ($0.3\text{ atm}$) and graphene monolayer thickness $t = 0.34 \times 10^{-9}\text{ m}$:

$$\sigma = \frac{\Delta P \cdot R}{2t} = \frac{30,397 \cdot 1}{2 \cdot 0.34 \times 10^{-9}} \approx 44.7\text{ GPa}$$

Since $\sigma = 44.7\text{ GPa}$ is significantly below the maximum theoretical ultimate tensile strength of ideal graphene ($\sigma_{\text{max}} \approx 130\text{ GPa}$), structural integrity is mathematically confirmed.

## 📄 Abstract

* **Background:** Traditional concepts for harvesting the Helium-3 isotope from the lunar surface rely on heavy mechanical processing, fine crushing, and thermal sublimation of millions of tons of regolith. These approaches entail critical energy costs, logistical complexity, and severe degradation of the lunar landscape. This paper proposes an alternative paradigm: space biomining (Biomining) based on an artificially engineered bio-hybrid plant system.
* **Methods:** We conceptualize a genetic hybrid combining the macro-structure and CAM-photosynthesis of the succulent genus *Opuntia* with the cellular resilience mechanisms of the extremophilic tardigrade *Ramazzottius varieornatus*, integrated with functional bacterial genes from *Cupriavidus metallidurans*. The hybrid is designed to grow under lightweight, transparent protective domes with minimal CO₂ pressure and closed-loop water vapor recovery.
* **Results:** To survive extreme temperature drops (down to –173 °C), the hybrid expresses tardigrade CAHS (Cytosolic Abundant Heat-Soluble) proteins, converting the intracellular environment into a protective vitreous hydrogel (bio-glass) that prevents ice crystallization. Radiation protection is achieved via the integration of the Dsup (Damage Suppressor) protein gene, which binds to nuclear chromatin and shields DNA from cosmic rays. Intracellular extraction of Helium-3 from the regolith is driven by the expression of lithotrophic bacterial pathways in the root system, causing biomineralization of heavy toxic metals into inert crystals, while inert Helium-3 gas is channeled via xylem to stem parenchymal cells. Gas containment is achieved by introducing bacterial gas vesicle protein complexes (GvpA and GvpC), forming rigid, gas-impermeable nano-containers inside the stem’s biological "sponge" structure.
* **Conclusions:** The proposed method allows for non-destructive, eco-friendly desorption of Helium-3. Biomass thermal processing at 60–70 °C denatures the protective protein shells, safely releasing pure Helium-3 gas into collection cylinders. This biotechnology establishes a closed-loop sustainable matrix for lunar colonization, bypassing traditional mining hazards and opening a path toward a zero-carbon fusion energy economy.
